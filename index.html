01 — The person

A mind that prefers the workshop to the feed.

Who this is, what he cares about, and why the work looks the way it does. Short, because the work should take the space.

I started the way most builders start: unscrewing things I wasn’t supposed to. Remote cars, old printers, a bicycle gearbox. The question was always the same — what moves what, and why? By 2020 that habit had a team name, SUMracers, and a world stage: RoboCross and the virtual World Robot Olympiads.

Now the loop is sketch → CAD → print → code → test. I model in Fusion 360, print on an Anycubic Kobra 2 Pro, wire ESP32 boards, and write the firmware — then document what failed. Competitions from Teknofest to WRO Azerbaijan taught the same lesson the bench did: constraints first, excuses never.

Cybersecurity (second place at the National Junior Cybersecurity Olympiad) and applied AI (honor roll at the International Inter-School AI Olympiad) joined the bench later. I also volunteer with Sumgait Youth House and Bir Könüllü. The aim is international engineering education — harder labs, stricter tolerances, people who build above my level. Until then, one prototype at a time.

04 — Philosophy, three lines

How I work when nobody watches.

01

Evidence over adjectives

A photo of the joint, a log of the test, a dimension on the drawing. Claims without artefacts don’t leave the notebook.

02

Small loops, fast truth

Cardboard before CAD. Breadboard before PCB. One variable per test, or the test teaches nothing.

03

Finish quietly

Document, label the box, put the tools back. Discipline is the part of engineering nobody photographs.
