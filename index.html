01 — The person

A mind that prefers the workshop to the feed.

Who this is, what he cares about, and why the work looks the way it does. Short, because the work should take the space.

I started the way most builders start: unscrewing things I wasn’t supposed to. Remote cars, old printers, a bicycle gearbox. The question was always the same — what moves what, and why?

Now the questions are larger but the habit is unchanged. I design small mechanical assemblies, wire them to microcontrollers, write the firmware, and then teach a simple model to make sense of the sensors. Hardware tells me when I’m wrong fast. That’s why I like it.

I’m interested in international engineering education not as a credential, but as a harder bench: better labs, stricter tolerances, people who build at a level I haven’t reached yet. Until then, I practice here — one prototype at a time.

“If I can’t explain how it fails, I don’t yet understand how it works.”

— bench rule no. 3, taped above the soldering iron

04 — Philosophy, three lines

How I work when nobody watches.

01

Evidence over adjectives

A photo of the joint, a log of the test, a dimension on the drawing. Claims without artefacts don’t leave the notebook.

02

Small loops, fast truth

Cardboard before CAD. Breadboard before PCB. One variable per test, or the test teaches nothing.

03

Finish quietly

Document, label the box, put the tools back. Discipline is the part of engineering nobody photographs.
