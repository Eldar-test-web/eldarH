01

WakeWell
A calmer way to wake.

Wearable · EmbeddedStatus — prototype v3 / ongoing

DomainSleep · Alarm · Haptics
StackESP32 · IMU · BLE (planned)
Made ofPETG · Silicone (test) · PCB
Year2025 — ongoing

Problem

Phone alarms wake with sound and light shock. For shared rooms and early training hours, that cost is paid by everyone. The question: can a small wrist device wake one person, gently, and know whether they actually got up?

Idea

A wrist-worn unit that reads motion and sleep-phase hints, then wakes with a staged haptic pattern instead of a buzzer. No cloud, no account — the logic lives on the device.

Housing v3 — clearance 0.3 mm, print: PETG · Dimensions & BOM — [to attach]

Design & process

Foam models first for wrist feel, then three housing prints (v1 too tight, v2 warped, v3 seats). Firmware logs actigraphy to serial; thresholds tuned from own nights, not from a dataset I don’t have.

Result / learned

V3 is wearable and wakes — staging still coarse. Learned: power budget dominates everything (deep-sleep regulator under test), and comfort is a dimension like any other: measure it.

K.O.R.A.

A personal assistant that stays in the room.

No server, no demo voice. A small offline helper: timers, bench conversions, log dictation, and waking the right script at the right hour.

# kora — local session, excerpt
eldar@bench:~$ kora log "housing v3 seats, 0.3mm"
→ logged 2026-08-30 23:12 · tag MECH/04
eldar@bench:~$ kora timer 25m --label solder
→ timer set. no network touched.
# intents: 003 reliable · 007 drafted · latency: [to measure]

Problem → idea

Cloud assistants leak context and die offline. The bench needs the opposite: a tool that knows five project folders and nothing about the internet.

Design → learned

Keyword intents + local scripts first; model later, only where it earns its latency. Learned: three reliable commands beat thirty theatrical ones.

AquaFly — a rescue-drone study.

Aero · Hull · ConceptFloat test 01 done · numbers open

Premise, kept small: a light VTOL sketch that can sit on water, carry a line or a small float, and survive being handled by cold hands. This is a study, not a product — hull first, rotors later.

DRAG / SCROLL — 003 FRAMES

Process

Paper sketches → foam hull → basin float. Weight log per gram; every gram argued for. Rotor sizing stays on paper until the hull floats right.

Differential-drive base, sensor ring, and the unglamorous part: cable routing that doesn’t snag, battery access without tools, code that logs why it stopped.

DriveDC · Encoders (test)

LEARNED

05

Security lab. Quiet, local, documented.

Networks · Linux · HabitsHome lab only — no live targets

What the lab covers

  1. Segmented home network sketch — what talks to what, and why.
  2. Linux permissions & SSH habits; keys, not passwords, where it matters.
  3. Firmware hygiene for own devices: signed notes, update path, rollback thought.
  4. Packet reading basics — understanding own traffic before judging others’.

Method & boundary

  1. Local VMs and own hardware only. Everything reproducible from notes.
  2. Findings written as checklists, not trophies.
  3. No scanning, testing, or “experiments” on systems I don’t own. Ever.

Boundary respected — lab-contained by design
